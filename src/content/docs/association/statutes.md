## Art. 1 Name and registered office

Under the name **TextRefs**, an association within the meaning of Art. 60 et seq. of the Swiss Civil Code (ZGB) exists with its registered office in Zürich, Switzerland.

The association is politically and religiously neutral.

## Art. 2 Purpose

The association's purpose is to advance scholarship, education, culture, and open digital research infrastructure by building, maintaining, validating, publishing, and long-term archiving an open, persistent, and machine-readable registry for canonical text references and associated metadata.

The association supports in particular the unambiguous, durable, and citable identification of works, citation and reference systems, canonical text passages, reference ranges, external identifiers, resolver targets, and curated mappings to external resources.

The association's activity serves the general public — in particular researchers, teachers, students, libraries, archives, museums, edition projects, digital infrastructures, memory institutions, and the interested public, both domestic and abroad. The publicly available registry data and documentation are open in principle, independent of association membership.

To pursue its purpose the association may, in particular:

1. develop and publish open standards, data models, schemata, documentation, and reference implementations;
2. provide persistent identifiers, landing pages, machine-readable metadata, JSON-LD documents, data exports, and versioned datasets;
3. receive, review, curate, and publish contributions from the specialist community;
4. ensure quality assurance, technical validation, expert review, provenance information, status models, and versioning;
5. cooperate with universities, libraries, archives, research infrastructures, edition projects, and other non-profit or public institutions;
6. provide training, information, and documentation offerings on open referencing of canonical texts;
7. accept donations, grants, subsidies, project funds, and other purpose-bound contributions.

The association does not, as a matter of principle, host copyright-protected edition texts, critical apparatus, commentaries, or translations from protected editions. It primarily publishes reference, metadata, mapping, and provenance information. To the extent that content is published, it must be in the public domain, openly licensed, or expressly licensed.

The registry data published by the association is, where legally possible, made available as open data — preferably under CC0 or a comparable public-benefit licence.

## Art. 3 No commercial or self-help purposes

The association pursues neither commercial nor self-help purposes.

The association pursues no commercial aims and seeks no profit. All funds of the association are exclusively and irrevocably dedicated to the association's purpose.

Any surpluses shall be used in full for the association's non-profit purposes. Distribution to members, founders, board members, staff, or persons close to them is excluded.

## Art. 4 Resources

To pursue the association's purpose, the association has in particular the following resources:

1. membership fees;
2. donations and voluntary contributions;
3. grants and subsidies;
4. project-related contributions from public or private institutions;
5. proceeds from events, publications, or purpose-aligned services;
6. other lawful income.

Proceeds from purpose-aligned services may be used only to realise the association's purpose. They may not be used to distribute profit or to favour individual members.

The amount of the membership fees is set by the General Assembly. The Board may propose fee categories and, for important reasons, may waive fees in whole or in part.

## Art. 5 Membership

Natural and legal persons who support the association's purpose may become members.

Membership grants no claim to exclusive use of the public registry data, no preferential treatment in the curation process, and no economic special advantages.

The Board decides on the admission of members. It may refuse admission without giving reasons.

## Art. 6 Termination and exclusion of membership

Membership terminates:

1. by resignation;
2. by death, for natural persons;
3. by dissolution, for legal persons;
4. by exclusion;
5. by non-payment of due membership fees despite a reminder.

Resignation must be communicated to the Board in writing. It is possible at the end of each fiscal year, unless the Board decides otherwise.

The Board may exclude members who act against the interests of the association, violate the association's purpose, fail to pay fees despite reminders, or breach essential association rules.

Excluded members may appeal to the General Assembly within 30 days of notification of the exclusion. Until the General Assembly decides, their membership rights are suspended.

## Art. 7 Bodies

The bodies of the association are:

1. the General Assembly;
2. the Board;
3. the Auditor or accounts review, where one is elected or required by law.

The Board may set up advisory expert councils, working groups, maintainers, review bodies, or other functions. These are not association bodies within the meaning of these statutes unless the General Assembly decides otherwise.

## Art. 8 General Assembly

The General Assembly is the supreme body of the association.

The ordinary General Assembly takes place once a year. It may be held in person, hybrid, or electronically, provided that the identification of participating members and proper decision-making are ensured.

The invitation is issued at least 20 days in advance, stating the agenda.

An extraordinary General Assembly is convened if the Board so decides or if at least one fifth of the members request it in writing, stating the agenda.

## Art. 9 Competences of the General Assembly

The General Assembly has, in particular, the following powers:

1. approval of the minutes of the previous General Assembly;
2. approval of the annual report;
3. approval of the annual accounts;
4. discharge of the Board;
5. election and dismissal of members of the Board;
6. election and dismissal of the President, where the General Assembly fills this function directly;
7. election of the Auditor or accounts review, where one is provided for or required by law;
8. setting of the membership fees;
9. approval of the budget, where the General Assembly so requires or a regulation so provides;
10. resolutions on amendments to the statutes;
11. resolutions on appeals against exclusions;
12. resolutions on the dissolution of the association and the use of the liquidation result under Art. 25.

## Art. 10 Decision-making in the General Assembly

Each member has one vote.

The General Assembly takes its decisions and conducts elections by a simple majority of the votes cast, unless the law or these statutes provide otherwise. Abstentions are not counted as votes cast.

Amendments to the statutes require a two-thirds majority of the votes cast.

The dissolution of the association requires a two-thirds majority of the votes cast.

In the event of a tied vote on substantive matters, the President decides with the casting vote. In elections, lot decides, unless the General Assembly resolves on another procedure.

## Art. 11 Board

The Board consists of at least three persons.

The term of office is two years. Re-election is possible.

The Board constitutes itself, unless the General Assembly elects individual functions directly. It may in particular provide for the following functions:

1. Presidency;
2. Finance;
3. Secretary or Recording Officer;
4. Data and Infrastructure;
5. Legal, Compliance, and Takedown;
6. Community and Partnerships.

The Board may delegate tasks to individual Board members, working groups, advisory councils, maintainers, or third parties. Overall responsibility remains with the Board.

## Art. 12 Competences of the Board

The Board manages the current affairs of the association and represents the association externally.

It is responsible in particular for:

1. strategic leadership of the association;
2. implementation of the association's purpose;
3. preparation and convening of the General Assembly;
4. execution of the General Assembly's resolutions;
5. admission and exclusion of members;
6. preparation of the annual report, annual accounts, and budget;
7. financial planning and oversight of purpose-bound use of funds;
8. issuing regulations, guidelines, and policies;
9. appointment and dismissal of expert councils, maintainers, review bodies, and working groups;
10. decisions on cooperations, grant applications, contracts, and project-related commitments;
11. ensuring data protection, rights review, copyright compliance, takedown processes, and conflict-of-interest rules;
12. decisions on publication, release, and archiving principles;
13. safeguarding the conditions for tax exemption.

The Board is responsible for all matters that are not, by law or by these statutes, assigned to another body.

## Art. 13 Signing authority

The Board governs signing authority.

As a rule, two persons with signing authority sign collectively. The Board may, for certain transactions, establish single signature, collective signature, or internal authority limits.

For transactions of significant financial or legal scope, a Board resolution is required.

## Art. 14 Board meetings and decision-making

The Board meets as often as business requires.

Board meetings may be held in person, hybrid, by telephone, or electronically.

The Board has a quorum when the majority of its members participate. It takes its decisions by simple majority of the participating members. Circular resolutions are admissible, unless any Board member requests oral deliberation.

Resolutions are recorded in minutes.

## Art. 15 Volunteer service, expenses, and compensation

Members of the Board serve on a volunteer basis as a matter of principle.

They are entitled to reimbursement of their effective, necessary, and documented expenses and out-of-pocket costs.

For special services by individual Board members or other persons, reasonable compensation may be granted, provided that it:

1. serves the association's purpose;
2. is at market rate and proportionate;
3. is budgeted or expressly resolved by the Board;
4. is documented transparently;
5. does not result in hidden profit distribution or self-dealing.

Details are governed by an expense and compensation regulation.

Affected persons recuse themselves from decisions concerning their own compensation or the compensation of persons close to them.

## Art. 16 Conflicts of interest

Board members, members of expert councils, maintainers, and other persons entrusted with decision-making powers disclose any affiliations that may affect the association's activity.

They recuse themselves in matters that particularly concern themselves, persons close to them, or organizations they represent.

The Board may issue a conflict-of-interest regulation.

## Art. 17 Expert councils, maintainers, and review bodies

The Board may establish expert councils, maintainers, review bodies, and working groups.

These support the association in particular with:

1. expert review of new corpora, works, citation and reference systems;
2. technical validation of data, schemata, and publications;
3. review of contested mapping statements;
4. documentation of provenance, uncertainty, and review status;
5. quality assurance, release preparation, and community contributions.

The Board governs establishment, composition, competences, terms of office, and removal in an organizational or data-governance regulation.

## Art. 18 Data, openness, and publication

The association publishes the registry data it produces or curates as open data, where legally possible.

Access to the public registry data must not be made conditional on association membership.

The Board governs details concerning licences, data quality, versioning, archiving, technical access methods, releases, persistent identifiers, and machine-readable formats.

## Art. 19 Scientific and technical quality assurance

The association ensures appropriate quality-assurance procedures. These may include in particular:

1. public change requests;
2. Git-based versioning;
3. technical validation;
4. expert review;
5. provenance information;
6. status models;
7. changelogs;
8. versioned releases;
9. archiving of dataset versions;
10. documentation of uncertainty and contested decisions.

## Art. 20 Rights, licences, and takedown

In its activity, the association respects copyrights, licences, database rights, personality rights, and other third-party rights.

The association does not, as a matter of principle, host copyright-protected edition texts, critical apparatus, commentaries, or translations from protected editions.

The Board issues guidelines on rights review, the treatment of external links and identifiers, the recording of rights and access information, as well as complaints and takedown requests.

In the event of legal or policy concerns, individual resolver targets, mapping statements, external identifiers, or other data may be marked as blocked, withdrawn, redacted, or otherwise restricted. Published reference IDs should, where possible, not be deleted but retained with a status and a justification.

## Art. 21 Financial reporting

The fiscal year corresponds to the calendar year.

The Board keeps accounts appropriate to the scope of the activity. Income, expenses, purpose-bound funds, donations, grants, expenses, and compensation are documented in a comprehensible way.

The annual accounts are submitted to the General Assembly for approval.

## Art. 22 Auditor or accounts review

The General Assembly may elect an Auditor or one or more persons to perform the accounts review.

If a statutory audit obligation applies, the Board ensures the election of a licensed Auditor in accordance with statutory requirements.

So long as no statutory audit obligation applies, the General Assembly may waive a formal audit and instead provide for a simple accounts review.

## Art. 23 Liability

Only the association's assets are liable for the association's obligations.

Personal liability of members is excluded.

## Art. 24 Regulations

The Board may issue regulations and guidelines, in particular concerning:

1. organization and allocation of competences;
2. expenses and compensation;
3. conflicts of interest;
4. data governance;
5. technical and expert review;
6. contribution and curation processes;
7. rights review and takedown;
8. releases, archiving, and persistent identifiers;
9. data protection and information security;
10. cooperation with partner institutions.

Regulations must not contradict the statutes.

## Art. 25 Dissolution

The dissolution of the association may be resolved by the General Assembly with a two-thirds majority of the votes cast.

Upon dissolution, profits and capital shall be transferred to a legal person tax-exempt on grounds of public benefit, with its registered office in Switzerland and with the same or a similar purpose.

Distribution among members is excluded.
